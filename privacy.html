Privacy Policy

SKAJ for iPhone and iPad · Effective 28 October 2026

In short. SKAJ keeps your logbook on your own devices. It has no account, no server, no advertising and no analytics, and it never sends your logbook to me or anyone else. The only ways data leaves a device are ones you choose: encrypted backups to a folder you pick (such as your own iCloud Drive), exporting your logbook, syncing with your own other device, or emailing feedback.

Who is responsible

SKAJ is made by Tuomas Kylmänen, an individual developer in Finland. Because your logbook never reaches me, I do not hold or control it. The one exception is any email you choose to send me (see “Feedback” below). Contact: feedback@skaj.app.

What SKAJ stores on your device

Everything you enter or import is stored in the app’s private storage on your iPhone or iPad:

  • Your flights and simulator sessions: dates, routes, times, aircraft, registrations, roles, landings, remarks and any photos you attach to an entry.
  • Names of the crew you fly with, if you enter them, and a directory of those names for autocomplete.
  • Your name, role and settings, and any historical hours you add.
  • Airports, aircraft registrations, routes and simulator devices, including your own additions.
  • Passport and visa records (expiry dates and optionally a photo). This feature is currently switched off in the app’s screens; if it returns, its data is stored the same way.

iOS protects this storage with your device passcode and encryption. SKAJ does not add a separate password.

What leaves your device, and only if you choose

Exports and backups

SKAJ backs up your logbook automatically. With a folder you choose once (for example in your own iCloud Drive), it saves a backup there by default every day, without asking. Otherwise it reminds you, by default weekly, and you choose where each backup goes: the Files app, AirDrop, email or another app. SKAJ remembers only that folder location. You can also make a backup, or export a CSV file or a PDF, whenever you like.

Backups are encrypted on your device before they are saved (AES-256). The key is kept in your iCloud Keychain, which Apple protects with end-to-end encryption and which brings the key to your other devices on the same Apple ID. SKAJ also shows you the key as a recovery key, after the first backup and any time in Settings, so you can open a backup on a device without your iCloud Keychain. Without the key, nobody can read a backup: not Apple, not me, and not you, so keep the recovery key safe.

“Export readable backup” creates an unencrypted copy (JSON) for an archive or another app. It contains everything in your logbook, so keep it somewhere you trust.

Safety copies

Before a sync replaces changes that exist only on one device, SKAJ saves a full copy of that device’s logbook inside the app (up to five from the last 30 days, and always the newest). These are not visible in the Files app. You can restore or delete them in Settings → Backup & Restore → Safety copies.

Syncing your own devices

If you pair two or more of your devices, they exchange your logbook directly with each other over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth (Apple’s MultipeerConnectivity), using encryption. There is no server in between and no internet connection is needed. Only devices you have explicitly paired can sync.

Feedback

“Send Feedback” in Settings opens an email addressed to the address above, in Apple’s Mail composer. The message contains the app version, your device model and iOS version, plus whatever you write. It is sent from your own email account and arrives in a Proton Mail inbox. I use it only to answer you and to fix problems, and I delete it on request. This is the only personal data I am responsible for as controller under the GDPR; I process it on the basis of my legitimate interest in answering you and improving SKAJ, and I keep it no longer than the conversation needs.

Links

A few links (for example to a company’s export page in the onboarding guide) open in your web browser. Those websites have their own privacy policies.

Camera and photos

SKAJ can scan a cockpit display or a crew list to fill in an entry. You take the photo with the camera or pick one from your library, and the text is read on your device using Apple’s Vision framework. The scan photo is never uploaded and is not kept in the app. By default it is discarded once the text has been read. If you turn on Save scans to Photos in the app’s settings (off by default), a photo you take with the camera for a scan is also saved to your device’s Photos library, where it is yours to keep or delete like any other photo; a crew-list photo shows your colleagues’ names. Photos you attach to an entry are stored in the app. SKAJ uses the system photo picker, so it sees only the photos you select, not your library.

Permissions SKAJ asks for

  • Camera, only for scanning.
  • Add to Photos, only if you turn on saving scans. It lets SKAJ add photos to your library; it cannot see or read your library.
  • Local network and Bluetooth, only to sync with your own paired devices.
  • Face ID, only before showing your backup recovery key. iOS checks your face; SKAJ never sees it.
  • Notifications, only for the reminders you turn on (recency, flights to finish, flight time limits). They are worked out and scheduled on your device; no server is involved.

SKAJ does not use your location, contacts, calendar, microphone or health data.

Third parties

SKAJ has no advertising, no analytics, no crash-reporting service and no tracking, and it contains no third-party service that receives your data. It uses a few open-source libraries (for reading Excel files) that run entirely on your device. If you have agreed in iOS settings to share analytics or crash data with app developers, Apple may provide me anonymous reports; that is Apple’s feature, governed by Apple’s policies.

If you buy SKAJ or start its free trial, the App Store handles it. Apple tells the app only whether you own SKAJ or the trial is running; I receive no payment or card details.

If you back up your iPhone or iPad with iCloud or a computer, iOS may include SKAJ’s data in that backup according to your device settings. That is between you and Apple. SKAJ itself never uploads your logbook; if you choose an iCloud Drive folder for backups, iOS uploads those encrypted files to your own iCloud account.

Other people’s information

You may enter the names of colleagues you fly with. That information stays on your devices under the same terms as everything else. You decide what to enter; please also follow your employer’s rules about what you may store.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays until you delete it. Deleting SKAJ from your device removes its data from that device. Settings → System → Erase All Content and Reset clears the logbook inside the app. Backup files and exports you created are separate files that you delete yourself. If you sync several devices, erase each device you want cleared.

Your rights

Because I do not hold your logbook, there is nothing for me to look up or delete on your behalf; you have direct control on your device. For an email you have sent me, you can ask me at any time to show or delete it, and you can complain to the Finnish Data Protection Ombudsman if you believe your data has been handled unlawfully.

Children

SKAJ is a professional tool for pilots and is not directed at children.

Changes to this policy

If the app’s handling of data changes, I will update this page and the date above before the change reaches users.
